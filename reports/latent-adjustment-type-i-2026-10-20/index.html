    Latent-adjustment Type I check — 2026-10-20

    Paper-grade size check of covariate-aware latent construction (covariate_latent_adjustment, motco analyze --latent-adjustment) at the Phase 6 SEA-AD design (openspec/changes/covariate-aware-latent-construction/, design D8, spec latent-adjustment-validation). It is the gate the case study must pass before it reports latent-adjusted trajectory results. Every number below is read from results/latent-adjustment-type-i-2026-10-20/ (latent_adjustment_type_i_decision.json, latent_adjustment_type_i_rates.csv, latent_adjustment_type_i_pvalues.csv, latent_adjustment_type_i_replicates.csv, merged.jsonl). Rates are rejection rates at p ≤ 0.05 over the available replicates of 500, at 999 permutations.

    Run: 500 replicates on ing (SLURM arrays 923443 and 923539, 50 shards, 5.1 recorded core-hours), from 979657c plus this change's working tree; file hashes are in PROVENANCE.txt. Decision: adopt_stage. The admissible conditionings are stage only.

    In one paragraph. Null datasets at the SEA-AD MTG design carried two covariates confounded with group × stage, whose effect was injected strongly enough that the covariate-blind basis spends its first latent component on the numeric covariate: median |Spearman| 0.83 between that component and the covariate. Each replicate was analysed with eight covariates declared, in three arms that differ only in how the basis is built. With stage conditioning, the three statistics hold the predeclared bound: delta 0.031, angle 0.067 and shape 0.058 against 0.0706 (R = 448). With group_stage conditioning, angle exceeds it: 0.082 against 0.0703 (R = 462). Residualization moved the first component off the covariate (median |ρ| 0.34). The case study may use --latent-adjustment stage under the conditions in §5, and not group_stage.

    1. Construction

    • Data and covariates. The covariate-adjustment check's construction (report §1), imported from scripts/covariate_type_i_check.py rather than copied. The data are generate_semisynthetic_trajectory at the phase6_small_n settings: three stages, group_stage_sizes = [[11, 10, 28], [9, 10, 12]], methylation (M-values) and expression blocks, 498 features, group_effect_size = 0. A numeric z and a three-level factor depend on group, stage and their interaction (a_g = 0.5, a_s = 1, a_gs = 2, logit scale 0.5).
    • The seeds are the covariate check's (base seed 2026). Replicate r therefore draws the same generated blocks, z and factor as that check's replicate r.
    • Noise covariates. Six i.i.d. N(0, 1) numeric covariates x1–x6, declared but never injected, are drawn from their own seed. Every arm therefore declares 8 covariates (9 design columns). This puts the residualization at the case study's 8–10 covariates and its inner CV folds at the case study's degrees of freedom (design, Risks).
    • Injection. X_f += s_z·sd_f·w_f·z + s_c·sd_f·Σ_k u_{k,f}·1[cat = k] on a random 20 % of features per direction. The strengths are s_z = 40 (calibrated, below) and s_c = 2.
    • Arms. Each replicate runs analyze_trajectories three times with the same covariates, CV settings (harness defaults) and RRPP seed, and the net-displacement estimator:
    • baseline: covariates in the trajectory model only, with the covariate-blind basis (the 0.8.0 analysis);
    • stage: covariate_latent_adjustment="stage";
    • group_stage: covariate_latent_adjustment="group_stage".
    • Rule. For each residualized arm and statistic, the rate over the R available replicates must be at most α + k·√(α(1 − α)/R), with α = 0.05 and k = 2. An arm is admissible when all three statistics hold. The decision is adopt_stage when stage is admissible, adopt_group_stage when only group_stage is, and otherwise diagnose_latent_excess. The baseline arm is reported only.

    Calibration

    The gate needs the covariate-blind basis to be spent on the nuisance axis. The criterion is a median |Spearman| of at least 0.8 between the first component's scores and z, checked on the smoke profile before any paper-grade run. At the covariate check's s_z = 3, the median over the 20 smoke replicates was 0.64. It rises with s_z and then saturates: 0.74 at 6, 0.78 at 10, 0.80 at 20, 0.81 at 30 and 0.815 at 40. The covariate-blind fit re-standardizes every feature, so once the injected features are dominated by z a stronger injection adds nothing; only the injected fifth of the features can carry z. s_z = 40 was chosen for the most margin. The smoke profile (R = 20, 99 permutations) confirmed 0.815.

    In the paper-grade run the baseline median |ρ| is 0.830 (5–95 % range 0.751–0.891; minimum 0.708), so the criterion is met. The covariate-blind basis selected rank 2 in 265 replicates, rank 3 in 233 and rank 4 in 2.

    At p < 0.05 instead of p ≤ 0.05, every rate is the same except group_stage angle, which is 0.078 and still exceeds. The decision is unchanged.

    Stability. In five consecutive blocks of 100 replicates (rates over each block's available replicates):

    • stage: delta 0.04, 0.01, 0.03, 0.03, 0.03; angle 0.03, 0.06, 0.07, 0.13, 0.04; shape 0.05, 0.07, 0.06, 0.04, 0.07.
    • group_stage: delta 0.04, 0.02, 0.09, 0.04, 0.04; angle 0.05, 0.10, 0.05, 0.14, 0.06; shape 0.07, 0.07, 0.08, 0.07, 0.06.

    In both arms the angle rate is driven by block 4, replicates 300–399. The covariate check's adjusted arm, on the same generated datasets, also had its angle excess in block 4 (0.11). The stage arm's 0.067 sits 0.004 below its bound; group_stage's 0.082 sits 0.012 above, and its block 2 is also high.

    Unavailable replicates. An arm is unavailable for a replicate when a CV training design loses full rank.

    • That happened in 52 stage replicates and 38 group_stage replicates (22 in both), never in the baseline arm.
    • Every one is an inner-fold training set short exactly one column, an absent level of the three-level factor. They are spread across the five CV repeats (repeat 1 most often).
    • Excluding them does not flatter the residualized arms. On the replicates stage lost, the always-available baseline arm rejects delta 0.019, angle 0.019 and shape 0.096 (n = 52). On the replicates stage kept, it rejects delta 0.051, angle 0.067 and shape 0.076 (n = 448), close to its overall 0.048 / 0.062 / 0.078.
    • The group_stage comparison is similar: 0.026 / 0.026 / 0.132 on n = 38, against 0.050 / 0.065 / 0.074.

    3. What residualization changes

    • The basis. The first component's median |ρ| with z falls from 0.830 (baseline) to 0.336 (stage) and 0.355 (group_stage). The residualized bases no longer spend their first component on the nuisance axis; what correlation remains is the stage structure z shares by construction.
    • The rank. CV selects rank 3 in 417 of 448 stage replicates and 457 of 462 group_stage replicates, against 233 of 500 for the baseline. With the nuisance axis gone, the CV keeps more of the stage structure.
    • Degrees of freedom. The full-sample residual df is 68 under stage (80 − 1 − 2 − 9) and 65 under group_stage. The smallest CV training-set df is 28 and 25 in every replicate.
    • What is removed. The per-feature partial R² of the covariates given the conditioning design has a replicate-median of 0.13 (median feature), 0.27 (mean) and 0.99 (95th percentile). About 18 % of features have R² above 0.5, roughly the fifth of features that carry z.
    • The baseline arm. Here the 0.8.0 covariate adjustment alone, on the covariate-blind basis, lets shape reach 0.078. That is above the 0.0695 bound it would face at R = 500, and its rule does not judge it. The covariate check's adjusted arm held shape at 0.034 with s_z = 3 and two covariates. With a nuisance strong enough to take the first component, the model adjustment alone is no longer enough for shape, and latent adjustment is. This is descriptive, not a verdict.

    4. Scope and limits

    • Mediators. Residualizing a variable that mediates the stage effect removes real signal. No simulation can tell a confounder from a mediator, because the data look the same; this check injects a pure confounder. Choosing and justifying the covariate set is the case study's job, in its own proposal. A technical variable that is itself downstream of disease stage, such as a cell-quality metric that worsens with pathology, is a mediator candidate.
    • Additive, linear effects. The covariate effects are additive and linear, and correctly specified in the residualization and the model. Non-linear effects and covariate × design interactions are outside the check.
    • One design. n = 80, two blocks, 498 features, 8 covariates with 9 design columns, and one injected direction per covariate.
    • angle is near the bound. stage holds angle by 0.004, with one 100-replicate block at 0.13. Treat a marginal latent-adjusted angle rejection with the care the earlier reports ask of angle near the translation control.
    • Rare factor levels. About 10 % of replicates lose an arm to a factor level absent from a CV training set, and which fold fails depends on random_state. Merge rare levels before analysis; the record names the failing repeat and fold.
    • No power claim. This report covers size only. Power, with and without a nuisance axis, is the subject of the separate power comparison.

    5. Consequence for the case study

    The decision is adopt_stage. The SEA-AD case study may report latent-adjusted motco analyze results with --latent-adjustment stage (MOTCO ≥ 0.9.0) under these conditions:

    • its own proposal names and justifies the covariate set, including why no residualized variable mediates the stage effect;
    • group_stage conditioning is not used: it exceeded the bound on angle;
    • the record's latent_adjustment block (conditioning, design columns, residual df, smallest CV training df, the feature R² summary) and its covariates block are reported beside the results;
    • categorical covariates have no level rare enough to drop out of a CV training set;
    • the earlier binding statements stand: the CV-selected rank is reported beside angle, and non-rejections are uninformative;
    • the power comparison §6 applies: latent adjustment is a sensitivity analysis beside the covariate-adjusted analysis, not a replacement for it.
