    Stage-effect sample-size scaling — report (2026-11-24)

    Change: stage-effect-sample-size-scaling (MOTCO 0.25.0, no package change; roadmap open item 14). Reads: results/stage-effect-scaling-2026-11-24/ (stage_effect_scaling_decision.json; per profile settings.json, rate_table.csv, paired_table.csv, cell_table.csv, rank_table.csv, accounting.json; saturated/reproduction.json; calibration/; PROVENANCE.txt, sacct.txt). Parent: the univariate baseline's stage_span profile (its settings, rate table and cell table, bound by sha256). Concept page: measurement visibility.

    At a weak stage effect the CV rank inflates, delta and shape lose power, and a rotation is read as shape. This run asks whether more samples buy that back. In the generator a design's information about the stage program grows as δ√n. So, if a weak stage effect were only a smaller effective sample, half the stage effect with four times the samples would read the same, cell by cell. Each new design is paired with a committed design at the same δ√n:

    • δ = 1.0 and δ = 0.5 at n = 320 and 1,280 against δ = 2.0 at n = 80;
    • δ = 1.0 at n = 800 against δ = 2.0 at n = 200;
    • δ = 0.25 at n = 800 against δ = 0.5 at n = 200.

    A fifth pair sits below the AUROC ceiling: a stage effect calibrated to the case studies' held-out AUROC (δ_c = 0.05 at n = 320) against δ_c / 2 at n = 1,280. The decision was fixed before any unit ran. It labels each of 53 pair-cells by Fisher's exact test on the production omnibus, with the per-feature fdr arm as the control of the pairing.

    In one paragraph. The verdict is mixed: 3 counted costs and 12 counted gains. A weak stage effect is not only a smaller sample: for some plants it costs more than δ√n says, and for others less.

    • The costs are rotations, all at δ = 0.5, n = 1,280 against δ = 2.0, n = 80. The smallest in-span rotation (e = 0.1) reads 0.250 against 0.820. The two out-of-span rotations, which production sees only through incidental routes, read 0.060 against 0.255 and 0.375 against 0.835.
    • The gains are stretches and bends, and rotations below δ = 0.5.
      • Stretches and small bends at δ = 1.0 gain 0.105–0.300 over their δ = 2.0 matches.
      • Small rotations at δ = 0.25 gain over δ = 0.5: 0.310 against 0.090, and 0.765 against 0.410.
      • At the calibrated weak signal, the n = 1,280 design gains on every stretch, for example 0.805 against 0.330 at e = 1.
      • The same δ = 0.5 design that loses the e = 0.1 rotation gains the e = 0.25 one (0.985 against 0.855).
    • The control moved once. fdr carries no label in 52 of 53 pair-cells, and its one label (a gain on an out-of-span rotation at δ = 0.25) is not one production carries. No label was set aside.
    • Sample size does not restore the naming or the rank.
      • The δ = 2.0 designs grade names_kind at n = 80, 200 and 500. Every weaker design grades detects_only, the matched ones included. At δ = 0.5, n = 1,280, a 45° rotation is read as shape in 0.720 of units.
      • The CV rank does not come down with more samples. At the AUROC ceiling it is set by δ: 3 at δ = 0.5 at both n = 200 and 1,280. Below the ceiling, n = 1,280 drives it to the cap of 19 at both δ_c / 2 and δ_c, against a median of 10 at δ_c, n = 320.
    • Size holds. Production, fdr and total hold size in both families. The rerun of δ = 0.5, n = 200 reproduces all 234 committed rows exactly. 24,400 of 24,400 units completed.
    • What it holds for. The reading holds at 658 simulated features with Gaussian noise, equal groups and three stages. The band designs match the case studies' held-out AUROC, not their cell tables, covariates or rank, and no real-noise reading exists on methylation, expression and proteomics (roadmap open item 2). The decision was written after a timing probe of two null datasets per design, at a seed outside the run, which saw no rejection rate. No verdict changes what motco analyze runs.

    3. The decision

    The order is gate, size, then the matched comparison.

    • Size. Judged per arm in two families: the four new saturated designs (m = 8) and the three band designs (m = 6). Each family uses the count-plus-ceiling rule (judge_rates), with a per-cell bound of 0.0695 at 500 replicates.
    • Labels. Each pair-cell compares production's rejected counts in the two designs by Fisher's exact test, one-sided each way, at 0.025 / 53 = 4.7 × 10⁻⁴:
      • weak_costs: the weaker-staged design's rate is lower by at least 0.10;
      • weak_gains: it is higher by at least 0.10;
      • unresolved: otherwise.

    The saturated matches' counts are the committed ones. fdr is labelled the same way, and a production label counts unless fdr carries the same label there. - Verdict. By existence over the counted labels. With 200 replicates per design the difference of two rates has a standard error up to 0.05, so unresolved is not agreement. - Same section, same limits: the reading holds at 658 simulated features with Gaussian noise, equal groups and three stages. The rule was written after a timing probe of null datasets (design, Context), which saw the rank, AUROC, capture and timing of two units per design and no rejection rate.

    9. What follows

    • The documentation states the map of costs and gains by plant family (design, Migration Plan, mixed). The measurement-visibility section says it:
      • Stretches and bends. For a weakly staged design with a matched sample, power follows δ√n or does better.
      • Small in-span rotations. Below δ = 1.0 they cost: at δ = 0.5 against δ = 2.0, sample size does not buy back a rotation at e = 0.1.
      • The incidental route to out-of-span rotations weakens with δ at fixed δ√n.
      • Power cannot be read off one number. A cohort's power is indexed by its stage effect and its sample size separately, not by δ√n alone.
    • The naming is not bought back. Every design below δ = 2.0 grades detects_only, at matched δ√n and at four times the samples. A weakly staged cohort's rejection names a group × stage difference; the statistic that rejects does not name its kind, whatever its sample size.
    • The rank does not come down with sample size. At the AUROC ceiling δ sets it (3 at δ = 0.5 at both n = 200 and 1,280; 5 at δ = 0.25). Below the ceiling, more samples raise it to the cap of 19.
    • The rank rule stays on hold. The design reopens it under weak_stage_costs with the costs in the inflated-rank strata. The verdict is mixed. The strata of the one in-span cost are recorded (§8.3) and attribute nothing.
    • What does not change. motco analyze runs what it ran; no default moves.
    • Follow-ups.
      • The same reading on real noise belongs to the first multi-omic ROSMAP or ADNI export (roadmap open item 2).
      • Unequal group dispersion and feature count belong to roadmap open items 8 and 15.
      • The δ = 2.0, n = 500 match (δ = 1.0 at n = 2,000) was not run.

    10. Limitations

    • Simulated features with Gaussian noise. Every claim holds at 658 simulated features, equal groups and three stages, with one noise covariance for both groups. No real-noise reading exists (roadmap open item 2).
    • The decision followed a timing probe. The probe ran two null datasets per design at base seed 90001, outside the run. It saw their rank, AUROC, capture and timing, and no rejection rate (design, Context).
    • The pairing is exact only in the generator's means. The design named three places where the scaling is approximate:
      • the pooled SD's between-stage share, largest at δ = 2.0;
      • the residual degrees of freedom (74 at n = 80, 1,274 at n = 1,280);
      • the nonlinearity of rotations and bends in e.

    The run reads their sum. The fdr control moved once, but it is saturated or near its level in most pair-cells (§5), so it rules out little of the pairing's own drift. - Existence, at 200 replicates. A rate difference below 0.10 is not excluded anywhere, and the 38 unresolved pair-cells are not agreements. - The band matches an AUROC, not a cohort. It matches neither case study's cell table, covariates or rank. The calibration's δ_c sits near the band's upper edge. - The rank strata are conditional on a rank the data choose, and the rank-2 strata hold 5–10 units. - One match was not run: δ = 1.0 at n = 2,000, against δ = 2.0 at n = 500 (cost, and a saturated reference).
