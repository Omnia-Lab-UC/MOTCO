    Net-displacement power comparison — 2026-10-14

    Paper-grade power comparison of the net-displacement orientation estimator against PC1 in the production measurement space (openspec/changes/net-displacement-power-comparison/). It follows the net-displacement Type I study, whose verdict (paper_grade_power_comparison) selected it. It changes no production default; it names the change that would. Every rate below is read from results/net-displacement-power-2026-10-14/{phase6,phase5}/report/ (column_decision.csv/.json, design_point_operating.csv, eigengap_stratified_power.csv, acceptance_report.csv), …/{phase6,phase5}/consistency_checks.json and results/net-displacement-power-2026-10-14/comparison_decision.json, except where a number is marked as computed from merged.jsonl (§9 gives the snippet). Rates are rejection rates at p < 0.05 over 500 replicates × 999 permutations (Monte Carlo SE about 0.0097 at 0.05).

    Runs: results/net-displacement-power-2026-10-14/phase6/ (SLURM array 920303) and …/phase5/ (array 920304) on ing · Code: db5ab69 (run and reports), 1ccded5 (consistency checks) · Column decisions: candidate_qualifies at both designs · Comparison: adopt_net_displacement.

    In one paragraph. On identical datasets and permutation draws, replacing PC1 with the net-displacement orientation estimator in the cross-validated stage-PLS space raises orientation/angle power at the construction maximum from 0.300 to 0.648 at the Phase 6 small-n design and from 0.840 to 0.924 at Phase 5. It also clears every mandatory control at the one-sided bound 0.0695, which PC1 does not: PC1's angle rejects the shape construction at 0.138 (Phase 6) and 0.796 (Phase 5); net displacement rejects it at 0.054 and 0.022. Anchor size holds (0.050 at both designs). delta and shape are identical record for record between the two columns, as they must be. The predeclared rule therefore selects adopt_net_displacement, and the follow-up is the production-default flip. Two qualifications carry into the case study (§7): at n = 80 the gain is concentrated at the construction maximum and, within it, in replicates where CV retains more than three components (0.996 there, 0.280 otherwise), so a non-rejection stays uninformative; and the Phase 6 translation control sits one rejection under the bound (0.068).

    The cross-design selection (scripts/net_displacement_power_follow_up.py) returns adopt_net_displacement iff the candidate qualifies at both designs. Both rules are advisory: they change no default.

    • Phase 6, gain: candidate − baseline = +0.348 against a threshold of 2 × pooled SE = 0.059. Passed.
    • Phase 5, no_loss: baseline − candidate = −0.084 against 0.040. Passed (it is a gain of 0.084, just over 4 pooled SE, but the rule asked only for no loss).

    Rank still gates power at the Phase 6 maximum. Computed from merged.jsonl (integration_metadata.selected_lv): at Phase 6 orientation e = 1.0, 257 of 500 replicates retain more than three components (as in the parent study).

    selected rank replicates PC1 net displacement
    ≤ 3 243 0.082 0.280
    > 3 257 0.506 0.996

    At e ≤ 0.75 every replicate retains at most three components and power is 0.084–0.178. The estimator raises power in both rank strata. The headline 0.648 is the mixture, and it depends on how often CV retains the group-specific variation the maximal construction creates, which the Phase 6 report found a six-donor change moves from 51 % to 8 %. At Phase 5 the rank is 2–4 throughout and power does not depend on it.

    5. Acceptance targets (baseline column, descriptive)

    From acceptance_report.csv. The profiles declare upper-bound Type I and specificity targets on the baseline column; the gate is off and the column rule does not read them.

    • Phase 6: all eleven targets met.
    • Phase 5: ten of eleven met. translation/shape in the type_i_baseline cell is 0.070 against 0.0695 (one rejection over), failing both type_i_control and specificity[translation, shape]. That cell is independently seeded, measured in the baseline column, and shape does not depend on the orientation estimator. It is a Monte Carlo excursion at the bound in a statistic this comparison does not touch, not an estimator effect.

    6. Consistency checks

    From consistency_checks.json (scripts/net_displacement_power_checks.py, checks version 2).

    (a) delta and shape are identical between the two columns. The estimator enters only _estimate_orientation, so the two columns' delta and shape cannot differ. Over 8,500 paired records per design (17,000 comparisons): 0 p-value mismatches at both designs. Observed statistics are bit-identical in 11,668 (Phase 6) and 9,221 (Phase 5) comparisons and differ in the rest by at most 9.0 × 10⁻¹¹ and 5.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ relative: BLAS rounding across separate processes, inside the checks' 1 × 10⁻⁹ tolerance and never enough to move a p-value.

    (b) The baseline column reproduces the parent studies. It shares their generator seeds, evaluation parameters and RRPP seed, so wherever a parent record of the same mode, effect and replicate exists, its seed and p-values must be equal.

    Checks history.     7. Decision and interpretability

    comparison_decision.json (rule version 1; inputs phase6/report/column_decision.json sha256 c980fafb…, phase5/report/column_decision.json sha256 bdc3135e…):

    • Phase 6 candidate_qualifies (gain); Phase 5 candidate_qualifies (no_loss);
    • verdict adopt_net_displacement.

    The verdict selects the follow-up change: flip SimulationEvaluationParams.orientation_estimator's production default to net_displacement in a separate change that re-pins the committed signatures, as default-joint-magnitude-construction did. Until that change lands, the default and every committed result stay PC1.

    What angle means changes. Under net displacement, angle is the angle between the two groups' net displacement vectors (last stage mean − first stage mean) in the latent space, not between their principal axes. It reads only the endpoint stages. The paper and the case study must describe it that way.

    Revised angle statement for the SEA-AD cohort (n = 80, ≥30 nuclei, RNA + ATAC), replacing the angle row of the Phase 6 report §8 once the default flips:

    statistic rejection may be reported as evidence? non-rejection may be reported as evidence?
    angle Yes, that the groups' net directions of change differ. Type I is controlled (anchor 0.050; Type I study 0.048–0.058 in every space). Unlike PC1, the size, shape and location constructions do not move it: 0.024–0.068 at every effect, all within the bound, against PC1's up to 0.138 under shape. The translation construction sits at 0.058–0.068, near the bound, so a rejection alongside a location difference warrants care. The cohort's eigengap no longer conditions the statement. No. Power is 0.084–0.178 at e ≤ 0.75. At the construction maximum it is 0.648 overall, but 0.280 when CV retains three or fewer components (243 of 500 replicates) and 0.996 above that. Report the cohort's selected rank beside any angle result. A non-rejection is uninformative at n = 80.

    At the Phase 5 design (n = 1200) angle has power 0.92–0.95 at every effect and is specific: shape → angle falls from 0.80 to 0.02. The orientation construction is still detected by shape (0.998–1.000) and delta (0.80–0.91) at Phase 5, and at Phase 6 by shape (0.464) and delta (0.354) at e = 1.0. Those are properties of the construction and of the other two statistics, which this change does not touch. The delta and shape rows of the Phase 6 statement are unchanged.

    from pathlib import Path
import pandas as pd
from motco.simulations.grid import read_replicate_results
from motco.simulations.study.spectrum import record_design_point

def angle_frame(merged):
    rows = []
    for r in read_replicate_results(Path(merged)):
        if r.phase == "type_i_baseline":
            continue
        rows.append({
            "est": (record_design_point(r) or {}).get("evaluation.orientation_estimator") or "pc1",
            "mode": r.cell_metadata["trajectory_mode"], "e": r.cell_metadata["effect_size"],
            "rep": r.replicate_index, "rej": r.p_values["angle"] < 0.05,
            "lv": r.integration_metadata["selected_lv"],
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

d = angle_frame("results/net-displacement-power-2026-10-14/phase6/merged.jsonl")
o = d[(d["mode"] == "orientation") & (d.e == 1.0)]
print(o.assign(high=o.lv > 3).groupby(["high", "est"]).rej.agg(["mean", "size"]))      # §4 rank table
print(pd.crosstab(*o.pivot(index="rep", columns="est", values="rej").T.values))      # §2 paired counts
