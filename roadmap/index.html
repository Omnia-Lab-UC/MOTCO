    MOTCO Scientific Roadmap

    Objective

    Develop MOTCO into a scientifically defensible workflow that can:

    1. detect whether groups follow different multivariate molecular trajectories;
    2. distinguish progression magnitude and orientation reliably;
    3. treat shape with an explicitly validated interpretation; and
    4. identify stable feature and pathway drivers after a global trajectory difference is detected.

    The roadmap prioritizes interpretability and validation before larger simulations or additional integration methods. The evidence for each statement below is in its dated report under reports/; the measurement visibility page explains what the production measurement can and cannot see.

    Current position

    Established

    • Methylation is converted from B values to M values before standardization and PLS integration.
    • PLS is the production measurement space. One pooled, stage-supervised PLS-DA fit, its rank chosen by double cross-validation with a floor of 2 (item 4), and delta / angle / shape measured in its score coordinates. Block weighting and sparse stage-PLS (item 17, keep_production) and orthonormal span coordinates (item 15, power_loss) were measured against it and not adopted. Re-read in simulation alone (item 28, scoped_candidates), production holds size in every design family and under stage-unequal noise. predictive_orthonormal and block weighting each qualify in three of five families, and both fail size under stage-unequal noise, so no default moves.
    • Magnitude is specific under the joint construction at paper grade: magnitude/delta 1.000 at the top effect, magnitude/angle 0.024 and magnitude/shape 0.000 (addendum).
    • Shape invariance is audited and corrected. Production shape removes translation, uniform scale and any orthogonal transformation; reflections are aligned away at every ambient dimension (unify-shape-reflection-policy, resolving audit finding F3).
    • Orientation is measured by net displacement, the harness and motco analyze default since 2026-10-15. It holds size at paper grade and raises orientation/angle power from 0.300 to 0.648 at the Phase 6 design and from 0.840 to 0.924 at Phase 5, removing the shape → angle leak (report).
    • Given a difference inside the stage-supervised span, the tests are sensitive: a 45° rotation and a half-step bend are rejected in 0.96–1.00 of replicates at five designs at 500 × 999 (item 13 (a), sensitivity_established).
    • Covariates in the trajectory model hold size at the Phase 6 design (item 2). At the case study's configuration (six covariate columns at n = 80) the Freedman–Lane null needs the opt-in df residual correction, which is validated (item 8, adopt_df).
    • Two tests cover what the span cannot see, each validated for size in simulation and on SEA-AD's real blocks: the split interaction test for differences outside the span (item 16, split_validated) and the per-feature interaction scan for sparse ones (scan_validated; by block, by_block_validated).
    • In simulation, the trajectory test and the per-feature interaction test are complementary (item 24, complementary). Each holds size (the per-feature test as a false-discovery reading or a pooled sum; its max-T form exceeds at n = 80 on three blocks), and each leads somewhere. Production leads on small stretches of the stage program and small bends inside its span (16 cells; e.g. a stretch at e = 0.05, n = 200: 0.815 against 0.235). The per-feature test leads on concentrated differences: feature-set surgeries, out-of-span rotations, and in-span rotations at n = 80 or a weak stage effect (21 cells). A case study reports both analyses under a multiplicity policy fixed in advance (measurement visibility).
    • Covariate-adjusted orientation attribution is validated on semi-synthetic data at the case study's configuration (item 11, adjusted_attribution_validated).
    • A controlled inverse-PLS study showed that latent interventions reconstruct reproducibly into feature space, with round-trip errors near machine precision; a 45° latent orientation change reconstructed as about 42° in feature space with negligible magnitude change. Latent orientation differences are different relative combinations of stage-associated molecular patterns, usually dense loading-aligned feature changes.
    • SNF is not intrinsically unsuitable for integration, but its graph-spectral geometry is not naturally aligned with MOTCO's Euclidean path-length and angle statistics.

    Not yet established

    • The feature-set surgeries are not geometrically pure, and the measurement barely sees them. Orientation's shape response (0.998 at paper grade) is construction-present cross-talk of a per-omic feature permutation, present within each omic block; its predeclared "projection-associated" label is refuted (diagnostic, §1 and §4), and the paper must not repeat that framing. The surgeries' orientation and shape plants keep only the random-direction share of their energy in the stage-supervised span (0.2–1.1 %), so their low power measures visibility, not sensitivity (stage-span pilot).
    • The Phase 5 orientation claim is PC1's, and it is conditional. Under PC1 at n = 1,200, ρ = 0, orientation angle power is 0.840 ± 0.016 at paper grade (report, §5); it straddles the 0.80 floor by eigengap tercile (0.754 / 0.886 / 0.880), and it saturates at 0.84–0.88 from e = 0.25, so the strictly monotone form of the power claim is unsupported. Under PC1 the angle null tracks its own observed statistic (slope 0.811) and is load-bearing rather than corrigible (report). Under net displacement the eigengap no longer gates orientation power at the Phase 6 design; the CV-selected rank does, and it replaces the eigengap as the covariate reported beside any angle result (case studies below).
    • Cross-replicate driver stability has no design that holds the driver set fixed, so it is not claimable.
    • Specificity of the other magnitude constructions. The former default all (methylation δ alone) moves angle (0.276) and shape (0.742) far past the control bound at paper grade. Its off-target response is the geometry of concatenating one block that grew with two that did not (diagnostic); joint is the remedy and the default since 2026-10-09, and the historical configs pin all. Specificity stays unestablished for all, extremes and shape_kind='magnitude', so a magnitude result must name its construction. The acceptance-target reader's default two-sided rule records joint's conservative controls as not met; the opt-in bound: "upper" agrees with the gate.
    • Power at the Phase 6 design (n = 80, three stages, two blocks). Only delta has useful power (0.542 at a 10 % joint size change, 0.956 at 25 %), and no statistic is mode-specific for orientation or shape (small-n report). Under net displacement orientation/angle reaches 0.648 at the construction maximum (0.280 when CV keeps three or fewer components, 0.996 above) and 0.084–0.178 at e ≤ 0.75, so a non-rejection of angle at n = 80 stays uninformative.
    • Limits of the span measurement. The statistics are measured in sheared PLS score coordinates, which leave a path-length residual for the planted bend at e = 0.5 (−0.48 % of A's path on the SEA-AD table, −0.59 % at four stages); orthonormal coordinates remove it but lose target power (item 15), so the scores stay. The rotation's off-target shape rises to the per-cell bound at 45° (0.070 at n = 200, 0.086 at four stages). How much of a real difference the span sees is not established (item 13 (b)): the latent plasmode on ADNI (item 23) read gate_failed, and its diagnosis information_limited (one cohort's spread exceeds ADNI's program energy), so no real-noise capture is stated; only upper bounds are recorded.
    • Real-noise readings rest on two exports, each at its own design, and neither is the intended data. Size has been read on SEA-AD astrocytes (80 donors on 10,000 features) and on ADNI's metabolomics blocks (1,501 participants on 298 features, item 21). Power and specificity on real noise have been read on ADNI only (item 22). There plants of the covariate-adjusted stage program were edited from the analysed sample, whose off-target truth is exact, or from held-out participants, whose configuration no fit was built on and is about 0.3 signal. They were read at 750 participants and the full cohort.
      • Production on ADNI's noise. delta reaches the 0.80 floor from a stretch of e = 1 at 750 participants (0.995 in sample, 0.855 held out) and from e = 0.5 in sample at the cohort (0.825). angle and shape reach it at no effect read (at most 0.40). Its off-target family leaks: in-sample rotations are read by shape up to 0.395, more often than by angle, so a rejection there names a difference, not its kind.
      • SEA-AD. The only in-span plant is a magnitude edit along the in-sample stage means, and the anchor's replicates share one fit. SEA-AD's anchor gave item 18 its verdict; item 19 traced that excess to the in-sample fit read under RRPP's unrestricted draws, and item 20 read the within-stage null as specificity_excess.
      • The candidate on ADNI. Production and predictive_orthonormal hold size at every design point (excess_absent), while the within-stage null fails at the full cohort and the scores exceed at forced ranks 8 and 19 (reference_exceeds). predictive_orthonormal then gains but loses protected power on an in-sample 90° rotation and leaks in simulation (power_loss, item 22).
      • Limits, not vetoes (the user's decision of 2026-11-25). The candidates' real-noise readings above stay recorded as they are, but no longer decide admissibility: the candidates were re-read in simulation alone (item 28). The SEA-AD excess has a simulated analogue there: where the largest stage has the narrowest noise, predictive_orthonormal reads 0.090 against a ceiling of 0.084, while production holds.
      • Scope. Under the scoping rule item 21 declared, each reading speaks for its own export and design points: neither cohort vouches for the other, and ROSMAP has no reading (open item 2). Both exports are convenience data (case studies below): the blocks MOTCO was designed for, methylation, expression and proteomics, have no real-data or real-noise reading on either intended cohort.
    • No real-data trajectory difference has been detected. Neither case study rejects any statistic (below). At ADNI's design the non-rejections are measured to be uninformative in simulation (item 9), and those of angle and shape on ADNI's own noise too (item 22); at SEA-AD's they are bracketed to be uninformative. Orientation-driver attribution has therefore not run on a real-data claim, and on SEA-AD's real blocks a planted orientation on 20 of 10,000 features is not detected at up to 3 within-stage SDs, because it lies almost entirely outside the stage-PLS span (item 12). Neither the split test nor the interaction scan rejects on SEA-AD; at its stage signal no part of the split test has power for any plant, so those non-rejections are uninformative too.
    • Against the default analysis, nothing is established on real noise, and production's statistics do not always name the kind of a difference (item 24). In simulation the trajectory test and the per-feature interaction test are complementary (Established, above). Neither has been read against the other on real methylation, expression or proteomics, which have heavier tails, mean–variance dependence and many more features than the generator's 658 (open item 2). The grade is detects_only: at n = 200 with a weak stage effect, a 45° in-span rotation is read as shape in 0.665 of units, so a rejection names a group × stage difference but not, at every design, its kind.
    • The simulated conditions cover a narrow part of the designs a cohort can have (recorded 2026-11-24; open items 15 and 17, and items 25–28). The paper-grade power readings sit at the core stage effect δ = 2.0, where the held-out stage AUROC is 1.000, and mostly at equal groups: n = 80, 200 and 500 at three stages (items 7 (a) and 24), n = 1,200 at four (Phase 5) and n = 200 at four, at δ = 2.0 and 0.5 (item 28, where production holds size).
      • Stage effect. Below δ = 2.0 the tests were read at n = 200 and on the SEA-AD table (item 7 (a)'s stage-effect arm, item 18's band, item 24's δ = 0.5 column), at ADNI's design (item 9), and on designs matched in δ√n to item 24's and calibrated to the case studies' held-out AUROC (item 25, mixed). More samples at the same δ√n buy back stretches and bends, and more. They do not buy back a small in-span rotation at δ = 0.5 (0.250 against 0.820 at δ = 2.0), the naming of the kind of a difference (every design below δ = 2.0 grades detects_only), or the rank. A cohort's power is therefore indexed by its stage effect and its sample size separately, not by δ√n alone (measurement visibility).
      • Unbalance. The SEA-AD table and ADNI's were read at their own designs; both hold size in simulation, the SEA-AD one under the df correction (items 8 and 9). Group shares of 0.65 and 0.8 were read for size at paper grade on null data (item 26, unbalance_excess), and its one failing reading was diagnosed (item 27, small_cells). At equal dispersion and δ = 2.0, every design at n ≥ 80 holds size, down to a smaller group of 5 samples per stage, balanced or not; the committed delta reading at n = 80, share 0.8 (0.088) did not recur on fresh datasets (0.045). The one failure is angle at n = 42 with 7 per stage in both groups (0.076 against a ceiling of 0.065), and no balanced design below 7 per stage was read. Power at those shares, and stage proportions of 0.5 / 0.3 / 0.2, were read at pilot grade only (100 × 199, n = 200, two blocks; pilot §3.3–3.4). The translation control read 0.02–0.08 there, magnitude moved little, and the orientation surgery at e = 1.0 rose with the CV rank (0.97 and 0.70 against 0.12). With unequal group dispersion every family of item 26 fails, balanced designs included; item 27's re-read reads them as dispersion_excess, at 13 samples per stage or more in every cell (measurement visibility). The SEA-AD table on three blocks (δ = 2.0 and 0.5) and the share of 0.8 at n = 200 were read with planted cells at paper grade in item 28, where production holds size.
      • Dimension. 658 features, 1 to 8 per sample, against 125 per donor on SEA-AD (open item 15).
      • Noise. Gaussian, with one covariance for both groups except in item 26's null-only run. There the second group's noise SD was scaled by 1.25–2 on every feature, and delta lost its size even with balanced groups; under item 27's re-read (dispersion_excess) a rejection is evidence of a difference in trajectory or in dispersion. Within-group draws leave that delta excess (open item 17). A dispersion that differs by stage in both groups was read for size in item 28 on ten null designs: production holds, and both candidates fail where the largest stage has the narrowest noise. A dispersion that differs by feature, a difference in correlation structure, and power under unequal dispersion are unread.

    Phases

    The original sequence ran from closing the baseline studies through the shape and construction audits, attribution, a medium pilot and the paper-grade study to a real-data case study, with SNF-native methods optional at the end.

    Phase 0 — Close and preserve the baseline

    Done 2026-08-05. The latent-space metric-compatibility and inverse-PLS studies are archived and their capability specifications are canonical. The production contract is recorded: pooled PLS-DA, M-value methylation, a fixed shared coordinate system and per-statistic interpretation boundaries. The July M-value/PLS pilot predates the corrected shape estimator and the realized-geometry diagnostics; it is kept unchanged as historical evidence.

    Phase 1 — Shape invariance

    Resolved 2026-08-04 (audit). Translation, uniform scaling and proper rotation have documented expected outputs enforced by tests. The reflection policy was revisited on 2026-09-03 (unify-shape-reflection-policy): reflections are aligned away at every ambient dimension, and mirror pairs score zero at full rank and above.

    Phase 2 — Orientation and shape constructions before integration

    Resolved 2026-08-05 (findings). Every study mode has a documented realized geometry before PLS, and evaluations record analytic population, standardized population, observed standardized and PLS-latent checkpoints. Orientation is pure in native methylation but mixed after the biological cascade; both shape constructions are free or mixed bends, not shape-specific interventions.

    Phase 3 — Orientation-driver attribution

    Implemented 2026-08-27 (add-orientation-driver-attribution; extended 2026-09-04 by align-orientation-attribution; API). One pooled preprocessing and PLS model stays fixed for both groups. Attribution reports the observed normalized feature contrast q_observed = d_B/‖d_B‖ − d_A/‖d_A‖, its PLS-reconstructed counterpart q_PLS and their residual, for adjacent transitions and for the signed principal orientations that decompose the principal-axis angle (with a relative-eigengap degeneracy flag). It adds bootstrap sign, rank and top-k stability within design strata, correlated-feature module and caller-supplied pathway summaries, and standardized and original-unit effects. Pooled VIP is context only. The exit gate, a reproducible driver report for a significant orientation result, is exercised in simulation from Phase 4 on and has never been exercised on a real-data claim (Phase 6).

    Phase 4 — Medium pilot

    HOLD 2026-08-27 (report; 1,900 units at 100 × 199, n = 300, four stages). All 18 Type I controls passed, magnitude delta was 1.00 and specific, and shape shape was 0.94–1.00 and monotone. Orientation angle reached 0.65 against the 0.80 floor with a flat curve. A sign-anchor defect was found and fixed but did not explain the shortfall (report). The pivotality study explained it: each replicate's angle critical value tracks its own observed angle, a load-bearing property rather than a defect. The configuration spectrum records, on every replicate, the eigengap that makes it resolvable, and the geometry audit set the preconditions for Phase 5. Two findings carry forward: driver reports must use the observed contrast, because the PLS reconstruction keeps about 6 % of it (captured-component top-20 precision 0.15 against 1.00 observed); and a space supervised on stage is sized for stage separation, not for the group orientation contrast.

    Phase 5 — Paper-grade operating-characteristic study

    Closed 2026-09-10 as a conditional pass (exit review). The design point is ρ = 0, n = 1,200, four stages, p_dmp 0.1 (design-point pilot), and the rank rule is stage-supervised double CV (latent-rank ladder, keep_cv). The paper-grade run (9,500 units at 500 × 999, 0 failures; report) met all three power diagonals and all 21 Type I checks and held the gate at HOLD on the two magnitude controls. The magnitude-construction diagnostic traced that to the methylation-only all surgery, and the joint re-measurement lifted the magnitude claim on 2026-10-08 (addendum; 4,500 units, gate PROCEED). Sample size and block count at three stages are measured at paper grade by the design-guidance slice (item 7).

    Phase 6 — Real-data case study

    Closed 2026-11-02 on a size-controlled null, with the attribution leg reported failed (exit review). The exit gate (collaborators trace each global trajectory claim to stable drivers and reproduce it from documented inputs) is recorded as not met as written. Reproduction is met; driver tracing is unexercised because neither case study has a claim, and the vacuous pass is refused. Covariate-adjusted attribution was then validated (item 11), but the plasmode on SEA-AD's real blocks did not detect a 20-feature planted orientation (item 12, attribution_leg_failed). Phase 6 is not reopened. The case studies are described below.

    Phase 7 — SNF-native trajectory methodology (optional)

    Deferred; the question is closed rather than pending (Phase 5 exit review, §5). It awaits a decision to define graph-native path-length and angle statistics, which is independent of the PLS workflow. Candidate work: group-stage states on one pooled fused graph; diffusion or resistance path length as a magnitude analogue; transition profiles or diffusion flows as an orientation analogue; normalized stage-by-stage diffusion-distance matrices as a configuration analogue; a permutation scheme that rebuilds or conditions on the graph without breaking exchangeability; and validation on controlled nonlinear manifolds. Exit gate: an SNF-specific statistic has a graph-native estimand, calibrated Type I error, controlled power and no claim of preserving feature-space Euclidean geometry.

    Case studies

    Both case studies are on convenience data (clarified 2026-11-23). Each dataset was already cleaned and under analysis, and was chosen to have real-data results for a symposium presentation. Neither is the data MOTCO was designed for: SEA-AD is single-nucleus data with RNA and ATAC only, and the ADNI export holds two serum metabolomics panels. The intended real sample is ROSMAP and ADNI with methylation, expression and proteomics, the blocks the simulation generates. Neither cohort has an export of those blocks yet (open item 2). Every real-data and real-noise reading in this roadmap is a reading for its export, not for that target.

Donor-level RNA + ATAC pseudobulks (TMM log2-CPM, top 5,000 variable features per block, chrX/Y/M removed); sex is the group and ADNC is merged into three stages (Not AD + Low / Intermediate / High).     How a case study runs MOTCO. Through motco analyze or motco.analysis.analyze_trajectories (API), pinned to the release that contains every option it uses. That entry point is the harness's production measurement on caller-supplied blocks (a regression test checks it against the harness on identical data); it defaults to net displacement, requires an explicit stage order and records the selected rank, null summary and spectrum. Do not assemble the analysis from motco plsr + motco de or from the stats primitives: they are a different pipeline, and the primitives and motco de default to PC1. Carry the structural-analogue limitation (InterSIM blocks, 498 measured features).

    Binding interpretability statement at the SEA-AD design (small-n report; angle revised 2026-10-15 from the power comparison §7):

    • delta. A rejection is evidence that the trajectories differ; it is consistent with a size difference but does not exclude a direction or shape difference. Where the groups' noise dispersions may differ, it is evidence of a difference in trajectory or in dispersion: in simulation a dispersion scale of 1.25–1.5 alone took delta above its size, balanced groups included (items 26 and 27). A non-rejection argues only against size differences of about 25 % or more.
    • shape. A rejection is evidence that the trajectories differ, but not of which kind; where the wider group is the smaller, or the stage fit is weak, it is evidence of a difference in trajectory or in dispersion (items 26 and 27). A non-rejection is uninformative at n = 80.
    • angle. The angle between the groups' net displacement vectors (last stage mean − first stage mean) in the latent space, so it reads only the endpoint stages. A rejection is evidence that the groups' net directions of change differ: Type I is controlled (anchor 0.050), and the size, shape and location constructions stay within the bound (0.024–0.068). Translation sits at 0.058–0.068, near the bound, so a rejection alongside a location difference warrants care. At equal dispersion and δ = 2.0 its size held at n = 80 down to 9 samples per stage (0.062) and failed at n = 42 with 7 per stage in both groups (0.076; item 27). Like shape, where the wider group is the smaller or the stage fit is weak, a rejection is evidence of a difference in trajectory or in dispersion (items 26 and 27). A non-rejection is uninformative at n = 80.
    • Selected rank. The cohort's CV-selected PLS rank is the covariate reported beside any angle result.
    • Group dispersion and unbalance (item 26, unbalance_excess; item 27, small_cells, re-read dispersion_excess). The permutation null assumes that the two groups share one noise dispersion. At equal dispersion, unbalance alone does not lose size at n ≥ 80, down to a smaller group of 5 samples per stage. Within readings at 13 samples per stage or more in every cell, a dispersion difference alone took delta above its ceiling in balanced designs and all three statistics where the wider group was the smaller, so the clauses above apply. A case study that rejects reports the two groups' residual dispersion in the scores, and which group is wider, beside the rejection (measurement visibility). Neither case study's dispersion has been read.

    Results to date.

    • SEA-AD. No statistic is rejected in any of the 44 analyses: the four primaries, twelve forced ranks, sixteen preprocessing variants, eight latent-adjustment arms and four donor exclusions (re-issued under df, MOTCO 0.13.0, 2026-10-31). The primaries' delta / angle / shape p-values: astrocyte cohort_30 (CV rank 5, AUROC 0.636) 0.501 / 0.449 / 0.331; astrocyte cohort_50 (rank 2) 0.628 / 0.242 / 0.514; microglia cohort_30 (rank 2) 0.705 / 0.456 / 0.991; microglia cohort_50 (rank 4) 0.834 / 0.291 / 0.644. The split test's complement, its one confirmatory part, is rejected in none of the 40 analyses that carry it (0.18.0, 2026-11-06; cohort_30 astrocyte p = 0.886, microglia 0.795). The interaction scan's global p-value, read at α = 0.05 under a multiplicity policy fixed before any real-label scan, rejects in neither cohort_30 primary (0.20.0, 2026-11-10; astrocyte 0.962, microglia 0.956), and no feature is a hit in any primary. The positive control fails on the primary basis in cohort_30 and holds on the latent_adjusted basis. The stage latent adjustment is inadmissible at this configuration (residualized_basis), so its angle and shape p-values are reported but not read.
    • ADNI. The primary selects CV rank 8 at a held-out stage AUROC of 0.600 and rejects nothing (delta p 0.112, angle 0.688, shape 0.711).
