    What the stage-supervised measurement can see

    MOTCO's production measurement fits one PLS-DA to the stage label on both groups pooled, chooses its rank by double cross-validation (floor 2), and measures delta, angle and shape on the group × stage means of the latent scores. This page states which group differences that measurement can see, why a rotation of the stage program onto other features can vanish from it, and what a non-rejection therefore means. Each claim links to the study that measured it.

    The latent space defines the question. A space fitted to the stages with both groups pooled asks: does each group traverse the shared stage program the same way? Its blindness to other differences is the scope of that question, not a defect of the tests. A different question needs a different space.

    The span principle

    The latent scores are a linear map of the standardized features, t = (x − x̄) R / s, with R the fitted rotations (one column per component). A mean difference d between the groups moves the latent stage means by dᵀR. Any part of d orthogonal to the span of R moves them by nothing. The measurement sees a group difference only through its component inside the fitted basis's span.

    The basis is fitted to separate the stages, both groups pooled, so its span is built from the pooled stage means. At rank K − 1 with balanced cells it is close to the span of the centred pooled stage means. The plasmode diagnostic measured this on the SEA-AD export: the pooled stage displacement keeps 99.94 % of its norm inside the rank-5 span (plasmode report, §4).

    Why an equal-length rotation cancels: the rhombus

    Take two stages. Group A's step is a and group B's step is b, of equal length ‖a‖ = ‖b‖ but along different features: B's stage program has been rotated onto other features, which is what the orientation surgery does when it relocates stage-changing CpGs. The pooled step is (a + b)/2, the group difference is b − a, and

    (a + b) · (b − a) = ‖b‖² − ‖a‖² = 0.

    These are the two diagonals of a rhombus, which are perpendicular. A basis built from the pooled step contains a + b and is orthogonal to b − a: in it, both groups' steps project onto the same vector, so the latent angle is 0 and the rotation has cancelled.

    Three or more stages need a stronger condition. With steps a_s and b_s, the difference at one stage must be orthogonal to the pooled step of every stage:

    (a_t + b_t) · (b_s − a_s) = (b_t · b_s − a_t · a_s) + (a_t · b_s − b_t · a_s) = 0  for all s, t.

    The cancellation is exact when B's steps keep the lengths and mutual angles of A's (the first bracket is 0) and are orthogonal to every one of A's steps (the second is 0): B's program has moved onto features A's program does not use, which is what a relocation does. A rotation within A's own features does not satisfy it and is partly or wholly visible. The stage-span rotation, made in the plane of A's own configuration, is the wholly visible case: it keeps 0.85–0.97 of its energy in the span (see the pilot table below). The latent-space comparison's alignment axis (span_alignment) moves one rotation continuously between these two cases at a fixed intended geometry (Simulations).

    The repository has measured the consequence three times:

    • The orientation probe's Phase 5 latent angle at full relocation was 6.1°, against a population angle of 90° (orientation probe, §4; the test still rejected, because the anchor's latent angle was 0.3°).
    • The plasmode's sparse plant kept 2.2 % of its norm inside the rank-5 span, what a random direction keeps (√(5/10,000) = 2.24 %) (plasmode report, §4).
    • Orientation/angle power tracks the CV-selected rank rather than the effect size (design-guidance pilot, §3.2 and §3.4).

    Which differences are visible

    A difference is visible when it lies inside the span, which means it re-weights the stage program both groups share:

    • Scaling the program (magnitude): B's stage steps are a multiple of A's, so the difference is parallel to the pooled steps and lies in the span. This is why delta is the well-powered component in every operating study.
    • Rotating or bending the program inside its own span: the stage-span construction (below) makes exactly these edits, and the measurement recovers them in the target statistic.
    • A constant offset (translation) is invisible to all three statistics by design. It moves the group main effect, which MOTCO does not test, so it is the negative control.

    A difference whose direction is orthogonal to the span is not visible, however large.

    Blindness is a direction, not a set of features. The question is where the difference vector points relative to the span, not which features it touches. The same features can carry both kinds of difference:

    • Re-weighting stage-program features along the program is visible.
    • Moving them in a direction orthogonal to it is not.
    • A difference on features the program does not use is visible to the extent its direction correlates with the span, which for an unrelated feature set is about the random share. A direction on a few such features keeps several times that share, because the sample scaler rescales them, and is still not detected (see the visibility profiles below).

    The three measured cases are all orthogonal directions. The relocation of stage-changing sites (the orientation and shape surgeries) points B's steps away from A's, and the rhombus places the difference outside the pooled span. A sparse plant on a few random features (the plasmode) points in an effectively random direction.

    The incidental routes, and their mixed signature

    An out-of-span difference can still register when the measurement departs from the balanced rank-(K − 1) picture:

    • Rank above K − 1. When CV keeps more components, the extra ones follow within-stage covariance and can pick up part of the group difference. In the Phase 6 study the latent angle at full relocation was 32.1° at rank ≤ 3 and 76.0° at rank > 3, with angle power 0.082 against 0.506 (orientation probe, §1).
    • Unequal cell sizes. The pooled means are weighted by the cells, so the rhombus becomes a kite and the diagonals are no longer perpendicular. The SEA-AD table [[11, 10, 28], [9, 10, 12]] is such a design.
    • Unequal feature scales. Standardization rescales each feature by its pooled SD, so a relocation onto features of a different scale changes the steps' lengths.
    • Correlated noise. PLS weights tilt toward the noise covariance, so the span is not exactly that of the stage means.
    • Nonlinear units. Methylation enters as M-values and drives expression through the cascade, so relocated sites need not keep their step length.

    Through these routes a rotation registers as a mix of delta, angle and shape, not as angle alone. In the design-guidance pilot, the orientation surgery at e = 1.0 moved delta (0.12–0.92) and shape (0.19–1.00) as well as angle (design-guidance pilot, §3.2).

    Score coordinates: the span is seen through a shear

    The statistics are not measured on an orthonormal projection onto the span. They are measured in the PLS score coordinates that scikit-learn's transform returns:

    T = X·R,   R = W·(PᵀW)⁻¹,

    where W holds the weights and P the loadings. PᵀW is unit upper triangular, so R is W followed by a shear. The scores span the same subspace as an orthonormal basis of the weights, but the map is not an isometry of it. Lengths and angles inside the span are not preserved: two configurations equal in the standardized feature space are equal in score coordinates, but two of the same path length or shape need not stay so.

    • Size is unaffected. Under the null both groups share one population trajectory. Any linear map keeps two equal configurations equal, and RRPP permutes inside the same coordinates. The paper-grade anchors and translation controls read 0.002–0.062 against 0.0695 (stage-span paper grade, §5).
    • Specificity is affected. A difference that is pure in the standardized feature space can move the other statistics in score coordinates. A bend that keeps B's path length exactly can still lengthen or shorten it once the span is sheared, and so leak into delta.
    • The residual is measured. The paper-grade leak decomposition measured the bend at e = 0.5 under the planted scale, where B's population path length equals A's exactly. The table gives the median B − A path-length difference as a share of A's path, on the same population rows and through the same fitted basis.

    The residual is largest on the unequal SEA-AD table and at four stages. At paper grade these residuals did not produce a delta leak (the bend's delta reads 0.016–0.036), but they are the one remaining route that the construction cannot remove.

    Orthonormal span coordinates (measuring on an orthonormal basis of the fitted span rather than on the scores) would remove this route. That changes the production measurement and is not validated: its size, power and reproduction of committed results are untested. It is held as its own roadmap item (roadmap, item 15).

    Measurement spaces that keep the rotation, and what they cost

    Every linear latent space is a projection of the standardized, concatenated features (concat). So it can only lose trajectory information relative to concat, never add it: a projection cannot see a difference concat does not contain. It can still have more power than concat, because it discards noise directions along with the differences it cannot see. In the orientation probe's Phase 6 design the shape construction at e = 1.0 was rejected on shape in 0.19 of replicates by pls_cv and in 0.00 by concat (orientation probe, §3). Choosing a space chooses which directions of difference count, and how much noise is measured with them.

    The orientation probe crossed five measurement spaces on matched datasets (orientation probe, §3–§6). At full relocation the population angle is 90°:

    space Phase 6 latent angle Phase 5 latent angle cost recorded by the probe
    pls_cv (production, net displacement) 56.7° 6.1° —
    pca at rank 2(K − 1) (label-free) 90.5° 90.2° Phase 6 shape power 0.19 → 0.00; angle leaks into the shape cell (0.39 under PC1)
    pls_within_group (two-sided, fixed basis) 90.3° 90.2° invalid without a refit per permutation: anchor angle 0.48–0.88; a refit multiplies the cost by the permutation count

    The label-free and two-sided spaces keep the full rotation because their bases are not built only from the pooled stage means. Neither replaces the production space: pca loses the shape test at small n, and the two-sided basis needs a refit inside every permutation to be valid. The stage-PLS space stays the production measurement.

    Measuring visibility: span capture and the stage-span construction

    Two opt-in tools of the simulation harness (0.15.0) measure visibility directly:

    • span_capture (SimulationEvaluationParams(span_capture=True), study key evaluation.span_capture) records, for every replicate, the shares of the population group difference (group_difference) and of the pooled stage means (pooled_stage) inside the fitted basis's span, beside the random-direction share rank / n_features. It also records the population means measured through the fitted basis (population_latent). The shares are energy shares: for one direction, the plasmode's 2.2 % norm share is an energy share of 0.05 %, equal to its random reference 5/10,000. See Simulations.
    • The stage-span construction (construction="stage_span") plants group B's difference inside the span of B's own stage configuration: a scaling, a rotation in the configuration's own plane, a bend at fixed size and direction, or an offset along the progression. It is circular by design, because it plants where the measurement looks. It answers how sensitive the test is to a difference it can see, not how visible the surgeries are. See Simulations.

    What a non-rejection means

    A non-rejection of angle or shape means no detectable difference inside the stage-supervised span. It answers the space's question (whether the groups traverse the shared stage program the same way) and nothing wider. It does not mean the groups follow the same direction, or have the same shape, in feature space. A rotation of the stage program onto other features, or a sparse difference on features outside the program, can be large and leave every statistic at its null. The span-capture shares below are the evidence for each simulator construction. Report the selected rank beside any angle reading, as the real-data analysis record does. The split interaction test asks the wider question directly.

    Testing outside the span: the split interaction test

    The production statistics cannot see a difference outside the fitted span, and no measurement space sees it without losing in-span power (above). The split interaction test (motco.stats.interaction.interaction_split_test; motco analyze --span-split-test) asks the out-of-span question without a space. It takes the group × stage interaction energy of the standardized features, in the fitted PLS model's input coordinates, and splits it exactly into three parts, each tested by a trace pseudo-F under Freedman–Lane permutation:

    • total: is there a group × stage difference anywhere in the measured features?
    • span: is there one inside the fitted span, where delta, angle and shape decompose it?
    • complement: is there one outside it, where they cannot see it?

    The basis is the fitted stage-PLS span. It never sees the group labels and is never refitted, so its draws are RRPP's at the same seed.

    "Span" means the fitted span, not the population stage program. At a strong stage signal the two nearly coincide: the fitted span keeps 0.90–0.97 of the pooled stage means. At a SEA-AD-like stage signal the fitted span keeps only 0.10 of the population program (comparison, §9). There a difference along the true program can fall outside the fitted span.

    Reading the parts.

    • A complement rejection means the groups differ by stage with energy outside the fitted span. It does not mean one group uses a different molecular program. Part of an in-span difference can reach the complement: the 3–7 % of its energy the fitted span misses, plus what the sample scaler moves there. A 45° in-span rotation is rejected by the complement in 0.35 (n = 200) and 0.32 (SEA-AD table) of replicates. At a weak stage signal the complement also contains whatever the fitted span missed of the program.
    • A span rejection means the difference has energy inside the fitted span. There delta, angle and shape say how it differs; the span part only says that it does. It is less powerful than the targeted statistics: magnitude 0.56 against delta's 0.74, a half-step bend 0.72 against shape's 1.00 (n = 200).
    • A non-rejection of complement or total is uninformative unless power has been shown at the design and for the kind of difference. On the SEA-AD blocks, a planted difference on 20 of 10,000 features at 2–3 within-stage SDs is rejected by no part above the null rate (0.02–0.05), as by angle. An energy test spreads over every coordinate, so a sparse difference does not register.
    • The in-span share (span_share) is descriptive. It must be read against the share a null analysis gives at the same rank and design, never against 0. Components beyond K − 1 follow the total covariance, where the noise's interaction energy also sits. At the plasmode anchor (rank 5) the null share is 0.24; at a two-component span at n = 200 it is 0.006.

    Why a ratio. RRPP permutes the reduced model's residuals, whose mean square is RSS/n, not RSS/(n − r). An energy statistic carries that shrinkage into its null, and summed over thousands of coordinates the null concentrates, so a few percent of scale becomes many null standard deviations. The pseudo-F computes its numerator and denominator from the same permuted residuals, so the scale cancels. At the plasmode anchor the uncorrected interaction SS rejects the null complement in 0.268 of replicates; the pseudo-F on the same draws rejects in 0.012.

    What it does not provide. No geometry: the split test says whether and where the groups differ, never how. It does not replace delta, angle or shape, and it does not find sparse differences. A group-aware space refitted per permutation and a sparse per-feature interaction scan are roadmap item 16's follow-ups (roadmap).

    Validation: split_validated (2026-11-05, report, 500 × 999 at the controls, 100 × 999 elsewhere, on the latent-space comparison's and the plasmode's own units):

    • Size holds on three simulated designs (the 18 control rates read 0.032–0.058, against a per-cell bound of 0.0695) and on the SEA-AD blocks at the case study's configuration (total 0.040, span 0.050, complement 0.012).
    • Out-of-span power. The complement rejects the 45° out-of-span rotation in 1.00 (n = 200) and 0.68 (SEA-AD table) of replicates, where angle reads 0.08 and 0.10.
    • Localization. As the rotation tilts out of the span, the span part falls (1.00 → 0.52) and the complement rises (0.35 → 1.00).
    • At SEA-AD's stage signal no part has power for any plant, and the complement holds size there (0.05–0.06), where concat's angle did not.
