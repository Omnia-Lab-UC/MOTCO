    What the stage-supervised measurement can see

    MOTCO's production measurement fits one PLS-DA to the stage label on both groups pooled, chooses its rank by double cross-validation (floor 2), and measures delta, angle and shape on the group × stage means of the latent scores. This page states which group differences that measurement can see, why a rotation of the stage program onto other features can vanish from it, and what a non-rejection therefore means. Each claim links to the study that measured it.

    The latent space defines the question. A space fitted to the stages with both groups pooled asks: does each group traverse the shared stage program the same way? Its blindness to other differences is the scope of that question, not a defect of the tests. A different question needs a different space.

    The span principle

    The latent scores are a linear map of the standardized features, t = (x − x̄) R / s, with R the fitted rotations (one column per component). A mean difference d between the groups moves the latent stage means by dᵀR. Any part of d orthogonal to the span of R moves them by nothing. The measurement sees a group difference only through its component inside the fitted basis's span.

    The basis is fitted to separate the stages, both groups pooled, so its span is built from the pooled stage means. At rank K − 1 with balanced cells it is close to the span of the centred pooled stage means. The plasmode diagnostic measured this on the SEA-AD export: the pooled stage displacement keeps 99.94 % of its norm inside the rank-5 span (plasmode report, §4).

    Why an equal-length rotation cancels: the rhombus

    Take two stages. Group A's step is a and group B's step is b, of equal length ‖a‖ = ‖b‖ but along different features: B's stage program has been rotated onto other features, which is what the orientation surgery does when it relocates stage-changing CpGs. The pooled step is (a + b)/2, the group difference is b − a, and

    (a + b) · (b − a) = ‖b‖² − ‖a‖² = 0.

    These are the two diagonals of a rhombus, which are perpendicular. A basis built from the pooled step contains a + b and is orthogonal to b − a: in it, both groups' steps project onto the same vector, so the latent angle is 0 and the rotation has cancelled.

    Three or more stages need a stronger condition. With steps a_s and b_s, the difference at one stage must be orthogonal to the pooled step of every stage:

    (a_t + b_t) · (b_s − a_s) = (b_t · b_s − a_t · a_s) + (a_t · b_s − b_t · a_s) = 0  for all s, t.

    The cancellation is exact when B's steps keep the lengths and mutual angles of A's (the first bracket is 0) and are orthogonal to every one of A's steps (the second is 0): B's program has moved onto features A's program does not use, which is what a relocation does. A rotation within A's own features does not satisfy it and is partly or wholly visible. The stage-span rotation, made in the plane of A's own configuration, is the wholly visible case: it keeps 0.85–0.97 of its energy in the span (see the pilot table below). The latent-space comparison's alignment axis (span_alignment) moves one rotation continuously between these two cases at a fixed intended geometry (Simulations).

    The repository has measured the consequence three times:

    • The orientation probe's Phase 5 latent angle at full relocation was 6.1°, against a population angle of 90° (orientation probe, §4; the test still rejected, because the anchor's latent angle was 0.3°).
    • The plasmode's sparse plant kept 2.2 % of its norm inside the rank-5 span, what a random direction keeps (√(5/10,000) = 2.24 %) (plasmode report, §4).
    • Orientation/angle power tracks the CV-selected rank rather than the effect size (design-guidance pilot, §3.2 and §3.4).

    Which differences are visible

    A difference is visible when it lies inside the span, which means it re-weights the stage program both groups share:

    • Scaling the program (magnitude): B's stage steps are a multiple of A's, so the difference is parallel to the pooled steps and lies in the span. This is why delta is the well-powered component in every operating study.
    • Rotating or bending the program inside its own span: the stage-span construction (below) makes exactly these edits, and the measurement recovers them in the target statistic.
    • A constant offset (translation) is invisible to all three statistics by design. It moves the group main effect, which MOTCO does not test, so it is the negative control.

    A difference whose direction is orthogonal to the span is not visible, however large.

    Blindness is a direction, not a set of features. The question is where the difference vector points relative to the span, not which features it touches. The same features can carry both kinds of difference:

    • Re-weighting stage-program features along the program is visible.
    • Moving them in a direction orthogonal to it is not.
    • A difference on features the program does not use is visible to the extent its direction correlates with the span, which for an unrelated feature set is about the random share. A direction on a few such features keeps several times that share, because the sample scaler rescales them, and is still not detected (see the visibility profiles below).

    The three measured cases are all orthogonal directions. The relocation of stage-changing sites (the orientation and shape surgeries) points B's steps away from A's, and the rhombus places the difference outside the pooled span. A sparse plant on a few random features (the plasmode) points in an effectively random direction.

    The incidental routes, and their mixed signature

    An out-of-span difference can still register when the measurement departs from the balanced rank-(K − 1) picture:

    • Rank above K − 1. When CV keeps more components, the extra ones follow within-stage covariance and can pick up part of the group difference. In the Phase 6 study the latent angle at full relocation was 32.1° at rank ≤ 3 and 76.0° at rank > 3, with angle power 0.082 against 0.506 (orientation probe, §1).
    • Unequal cell sizes. The pooled means are weighted by the cells, so the rhombus becomes a kite and the diagonals are no longer perpendicular. The SEA-AD table [[11, 10, 28], [9, 10, 12]] is such a design.
    • Unequal feature scales. Standardization rescales each feature by its pooled SD, so a relocation onto features of a different scale changes the steps' lengths.
    • Correlated noise. PLS weights tilt toward the noise covariance, so the span is not exactly that of the stage means.
    • Nonlinear units. Methylation enters as M-values and drives expression through the cascade, so relocated sites need not keep their step length.

    Through these routes a rotation registers as a mix of delta, angle and shape, not as angle alone. In the design-guidance pilot, the orientation surgery at e = 1.0 moved delta (0.12–0.92) and shape (0.19–1.00) as well as angle (design-guidance pilot, §3.2).

    Score coordinates: the span is seen through a shear

    The statistics are not measured on an orthonormal projection onto the span. They are measured in the PLS score coordinates that scikit-learn's transform returns:

    T = X·R,   R = W·(PᵀW)⁻¹,

    where W holds the weights and P the loadings. PᵀW is unit upper triangular, so R is W followed by a shear. The scores span the same subspace as an orthonormal basis of the weights, but the map is not an isometry of it. Lengths and angles inside the span are not preserved: two configurations equal in the standardized feature space are equal in score coordinates, but two of the same path length or shape need not stay so.

    • Size is unaffected. Under the null both groups share one population trajectory. Any linear map keeps two equal configurations equal, and RRPP permutes inside the same coordinates. The paper-grade anchors and translation controls read 0.002–0.062 against 0.0695 (stage-span paper grade, §5).
    • Specificity is affected. A difference that is pure in the standardized feature space can move the other statistics in score coordinates. A bend that keeps B's path length exactly can still lengthen or shorten it once the span is sheared, and so leak into delta.
    • The residual is measured. The paper-grade leak decomposition measured the bend at e = 0.5 under the planted scale, where B's population path length equals A's exactly. The table gives the median B − A path-length difference as a share of A's path, on the same population rows and through the same fitted basis.

    Orthonormal span coordinates: measured, not adopted

    Orthonormal span coordinates measure on X_in·Q, with Q = qr(R) an orthonormal basis of the fitted span (equal to W up to column signs), rather than on the scores. delta, angle and shape are invariant to any orthogonal map, so every orthonormal basis of the span gives the same statistics and p-values.

    MOTCO 0.19.0 offers them in the harness as the opt-in SimulationEvaluationParams.latent_coordinates = "orthonormal". The setting keeps the fit, rank and permutation draws. It is not available in motco analyze, in study configurations or with attribution. Without it every output is unchanged.

    The 2026-11-09 validation measured both coordinate systems on the same units: the stage-span paper grade's 32,500 units and the weak design's 1,000 controls, with the same data, fit and draws, at 500 × 999. Its verdict is power_loss.

    • Size holds. The orthonormal coordinates read 0.000–0.066 at the 60 paper-grade controls and 0.010–0.050 at the weak design's 6, none above its bound.
    • The leak is removed. The watched cells are the 45° rotation's off-target shape: 0.070 → 0.000 at n = 200 and 0.086 → 0.000 at four stages (paired p ≤ 6e-11). The bend's planted-scale residual stays within 0.11 % of A's path, against −0.38 % and −0.60 % in scores on the SEA-AD table and at four stages. No off-target rate rises, and 1 of 90 is above the bound (6 are allowed).
    • Target power is lost. 19 of 45 target cells lose, 16 of them beyond the Bonferroni ceiling. All of them are at n = 80, on the SEA-AD table and at four stages, except two at n = 200. The largest losses:
    • the SEA-AD table's 45° rotation, angle 0.962 → 0.760;
    • the 22.5° rotation at four stages, 0.744 → 0.519;
    • the e = 0.25 bend at n = 80, shape 0.724 → 0.518;
    • the e = 0.1 magnitude on the SEA-AD table, delta 0.542 → 0.416.

    The three-block column loses nothing. - Why the scores have more power is open. The scores do not see a larger target orientation or shape: the population angle and shape are equal in both coordinate systems. They do stretch a planted magnitude by up to 5 %. Where the orthonormal coordinates lose, their angle null is wider: at the SEA-AD anchor the 95th percentile is 34.6° against 26.1°.

    Production and motco analyze stay on score coordinates. The scores' shear is a measured trade-off. It lets a 45° rotation leak into shape at up to 0.086, and it buys up to 0.23 of target power at small, unequal or four-stage designs. An off-target shape or delta rejection beside a strong angle or shape rejection can be the shear's.

    Only an adopt_orthonormal verdict would have permitted a separate change to adopt orthonormal coordinates. This run did not return it (roadmap, item 15).

    Measurement spaces that keep the rotation, and what they cost

    Every linear latent space is a projection of the standardized, concatenated features (concat). So it can only lose trajectory information relative to concat, never add it: a projection cannot see a difference concat does not contain. It can still have more power than concat, because it discards noise directions along with the differences it cannot see. In the orientation probe's Phase 6 design the shape construction at e = 1.0 was rejected on shape in 0.19 of replicates by pls_cv and in 0.00 by concat (orientation probe, §3). Choosing a space chooses which directions of difference count, and how much noise is measured with them.

    The orientation probe crossed five measurement spaces on matched datasets (orientation probe, §3–§6). At full relocation the population angle is 90°:

    space Phase 6 latent angle Phase 5 latent angle cost recorded by the probe
    pls_cv (production, net displacement) 56.7° 6.1° —
    pca at rank 2(K − 1) (label-free) 90.5° 90.2° Phase 6 shape power 0.19 → 0.00; angle leaks into the shape cell (0.39 under PC1)
    pls_within_group (two-sided, fixed basis) 90.3° 90.2° invalid without a refit per permutation: anchor angle 0.48–0.88; a refit multiplies the cost by the permutation count

    The label-free and two-sided spaces keep the full rotation because their bases are not built only from the pooled stage means. Neither replaces the production space: pca loses the shape test at small n, and the two-sided basis needs a refit inside every permutation to be valid. The stage-PLS space stays the production measurement.

    Measuring visibility: span capture and the stage-span construction

    Two opt-in tools of the simulation harness (0.15.0) measure visibility directly:

    • span_capture (SimulationEvaluationParams(span_capture=True), study key evaluation.span_capture) records, for every replicate, the shares of the population group difference (group_difference) and of the pooled stage means (pooled_stage) inside the fitted basis's span, beside the random-direction share rank / n_features. It also records the population means measured through the fitted basis (population_latent). The shares are energy shares: for one direction, the plasmode's 2.2 % norm share is an energy share of 0.05 %, equal to its random reference 5/10,000. See Simulations.
    • The stage-span construction (construction="stage_span") plants group B's difference inside the span of B's own stage configuration: a scaling, a rotation in the configuration's own plane, a bend at fixed size and direction, or an offset along the progression. It is circular by design, because it plants where the measurement looks. It answers how sensitive the test is to a difference it can see, not how visible the surgeries are. See Simulations.

    What a non-rejection means

    A non-rejection of angle or shape means no detectable difference inside the stage-supervised span. It answers the space's question (whether the groups traverse the shared stage program the same way) and nothing wider. It does not mean the groups follow the same direction, or have the same shape, in feature space. A rotation of the stage program onto other features, or a sparse difference on features outside the program, can be large and leave every statistic at its null. The span-capture shares below are the evidence for each simulator construction. Report the selected rank beside any angle reading, as the real-data analysis record does. The split interaction test asks the wider question directly.

    Which stage-supervised space: weighting and sparsity

    The latent-space comparison kept the stage-PLS span as the production measurement. It also showed how little of the stage program that span keeps at SEA-AD's stage signal (0.10 at the weak design). Two opt-in settings change the fitted span without changing its stage objective:

    • Block weighting (block_weighting: equal_variance; API) multiplies each standardized block by 1/√p_b. Unweighted, a block's share of the joint variance, and so of every stage-PLS direction, grows with its feature count; weighted, each block has unit total variance. The fit then runs without per-feature rescaling, so delta is in the weighted metric. When the blocks have equal feature counts the weights are equal, and the only change is that sklearn no longer re-standardizes each CV fold.
    • Sparse stage-PLS (sparsity: {scope: block}; API) lets each component load on a budget of features per block, chosen with the rank by the same held-out AUROC. A budget can drop features that carry only noise, at the price of any stage signal they carry.

    Neither setting can see outside the stage span; both can only change which stage-supervised span is fitted. The production latent-space selection (2026-11-10, report) compared them with production on matched units: the latent-space comparison's three designs and the SEA-AD plasmode export with a stage-span plant on real noise. Its predeclared decision is keep_production:

    • No gain where production has none. At the weak design every arm rejects at 0.01–0.07 and keeps 0.08–0.10 of the stage program at the anchor. On the plasmode plant the largest gain, sparse_block at e = 0.5 (delta 0.51 → 0.62), misses the Bonferroni threshold. weighted reads no_gain.
    • Size. Every arm holds size in simulation (0.00–0.06). On the plasmode anchor the sparse arms read delta 0.11, above the ceiling at 100 replicates, and production reads 0.11 on the same replicates. The decision counts it as size_excess for the sparse arms; the paper grade would have resolved it.
    • A gain at a strong stage signal. At δ = 2.0 every candidate rejects more often than production where production is below saturation: angle 0.46 → 0.90–1.00 for the SEA-AD table's 22.5° rotation. Production's CV picks rank 3 in those cells and the candidates rank 2, while every arm keeps 0.92–0.98 of the stage program. The gain follows the selected rank, not a better view of the program.

    Both settings stay opt-in. A case study may declare block weighting as a sensitivity analysis, with delta in the weighted metric. Sparse stage-PLS costs seven to nine production fits and has shown no gain that would justify it.

    The measured dimension: fit rank against predictive subspace

    The production statistics are computed in as many dimensions as the cross-validation selects components. The rank A does two jobs: it regularizes the stage fit, and it sets the dimension of the measurement. Dimensions beyond the stage program cost power on what the span sees:

    • ranks above K − 1 lose shape power at the Phase 5 design (rank ladder);
    • two near-empty added components halve magnitude delta (the latent-space comparison, 0.74 → 0.37 at n = 200);
    • the production selection's candidates gained where they chose rank 2 and production's CV chose 3.

    The predictive subspace. The stage labels are one-hot, so the fit's Y loadings have rank K − 1 whatever A is, and the fitted stage labels move only along K − 1 directions of the score space: the row space of the Y loadings. Measuring there keeps the fit, the CV and the rank, and drops the score directions that do not move the fitted labels. At A ≤ K − 1 it is the score measurement. MOTCO 0.24.0 offers three coordinate systems on that subspace as an opt-in setting, in the harness and in motco.analysis (latent_coordinates; API, analysis):

    • predictive: the scores projected on the subspace;
    • predictive_orthonormal: the input coordinates on an orthonormal basis of it;
    • predictive_fitted: the fitted stage indicators.

    Why a lower fit rank is not the same thing. Forcing the fit to K − 1 components truncates it, and the later components are what aim the K − 1 directions. In the predictive-subspace pilot (report §7) the forced rank-2 fit keeps 0.34–0.48 of the pooled stage program in the stage-effect band, against 0.73–0.88 for production and 0.67–0.87 for the predictive subspace. It loses the power the subspace gains: the n = 200 rotation at δ = 0.5 and e = 0.1 reads 0.11, against production's 0.06 and the predictive subspace's 0.94.

    What the predictive subspace gives up.

    • What production sees through its extra components. The Phase 6 feature-set orientation surgery at e = 1.0 is read by production's extra components (group_difference capture 0.20 at a median rank of 4), and by the predictive subspace barely (capture 0.002). Production reads its angle at 0.66 and the predictive arms at 0.48–0.49.
    • The size of the difference in the band. At n = 200 and δ = 0.25 the predictive arms read a 45° rotation as 18–20° where the population reads 43–45° through the same map. At δ = 2.0 they agree within about 4°.

    The pilot's verdict: keep_production. All three coordinate systems read size_excess.

    • Size fails on real noise. On the SEA-AD plasmode's anchor (500 permuted-label replicates, the case-study configuration) they reject delta at 0.13–0.136 and shape at 0.076–0.078, where production reads 0.042 and 0.026 on the same replicates. Size holds at every simulated design, the weak one included at 500 replicates.
    • No protected power is lost, and the gains are large. All three gain in 10 of the 18 δ = 2.0 target cells at the Bonferroni threshold: the SEA-AD table's 22.5° rotation goes from 0.41 to 1.00. In the stage-effect band, where the CV rank grows to 4–11 while the held-out AUROC stays at its ceiling, they gain in 29–30 of 36 target cells. On the plasmode's real-noise plant delta goes from 0.51 to 0.79–0.82 at e = 0.5.
    • Specificity depends on the metric. predictive adds to production's rotation-into-shape leak in the band, and predictive_fitted leaks at δ = 2.0. predictive_orthonormal passes both families and removes most of production's own band leak: 0.80 → 0.41 and 0.57 → 0.04 at n = 200, δ = 0.5.
    • The CV rank sits next to a leak. One more fit component turns the 45° rotation's off-target shape at δ = 2.0 from 0.03–0.04 into 0.92–0.99.

    Production and motco analyze therefore measure in the fitted span's scores. The predictive values stay opt-in diagnostics and are not tests a case study should report. Only a paper grade after a propose_paper_grade verdict could adopt another measurement, and that needs the real-noise excess understood first.

    The real-noise size excess, diagnosed

    A diagnosis re-read the pilot's 800 plasmode units with their labels and RRPP seeds (report). Its four reference arms reproduced the pilot's counts exactly. The guess under test: the two predictive directions of the rank-5 fit hold its in-sample stage fit (stage R² 0.84 against a held-out AUROC of 0.636), and the reduced model's residuals in them are not exchangeable across stages, which RRPP's draws assume. Two checks separate the parts of that guess:

    • Within-stage draws change the null and keep the coordinates. RRPP's residuals are permuted among the donors of each stage only. If the excess comes from mixing residuals across stages, it goes away.
    • Out-of-fold coordinates change the coordinates and keep the null. Each donor's fitted stage indicators come from a stage-PLS model fitted without that donor. If the excess needs coordinates fitted on the donors they measure, it goes away.

    Verdict: both_restore. Each check alone brings the anchor rates within the size rule the pilot applied. Rejections in 500 anchor units for delta / angle / shape:

    coordinates pilot (unrestricted draws) within-stage draws
    predictive 68 / 11 / 38 31 / 17 / 33
    predictive_orthonormal 67 / 9 / 38 29 / 17 / 32
    predictive_fitted 65 / 10 / 39 28 / 17 / 33
    out-of-fold fitted indicators — 8 / 7 / 17 (unrestricted draws)
    production (pls_cv) 21 / 24 / 13 19 / 27 / 19

    Three readings qualify the verdict:

    • shape holds by a small margin. The within-stage draws remove the whole delta excess, but little of the shape excess, which stays near the size bound and above production's rate.
    • The out-of-fold coordinates carry almost no stage signal. They hold size, but out of fold the stage fit explains about 2 % of the stage indicators' variance at the anchor.
    • Production gains under the same draws. On the real-noise plant, production under within-stage draws gains about as much delta power as the predictive coordinates keep. A comparison of measurement spaces therefore has to use the same null for both.

    The predictive values stay opt-in diagnostics; the diagnosis validates no test. The verdict opens a separate change: within-stage draws as an opt-in option of RRPP, the harness and motco analyze, with their own size and power readings in simulation and on this anchor. Only that change's verdict could re-read predictive_orthonormal.

    Testing outside the span: the split interaction test

    The production statistics cannot see a difference outside the fitted span, and no measurement space sees it without losing in-span power (above). The split interaction test (motco.stats.interaction.interaction_split_test; motco analyze --span-split-test) asks the out-of-span question without a space. It takes the group × stage interaction energy of the standardized features, in the fitted PLS model's input coordinates, and splits it exactly into three parts, each tested by a trace pseudo-F under Freedman–Lane permutation:

    • total: is there a group × stage difference anywhere in the measured features?
    • span: is there one inside the fitted span, where delta, angle and shape decompose it?
    • complement: is there one outside it, where they cannot see it?

    The basis is the fitted stage-PLS span. It never sees the group labels and is never refitted, so its draws are RRPP's at the same seed.

    "Span" means the fitted span, not the population stage program. At a strong stage signal the two nearly coincide: the fitted span keeps 0.90–0.97 of the pooled stage means. At a SEA-AD-like stage signal the fitted span keeps only 0.10 of the population program (comparison, §9). There a difference along the true program can fall outside the fitted span.

    Reading the parts.

    • A complement rejection means the groups differ by stage with energy outside the fitted span. It does not mean one group uses a different molecular program. Part of an in-span difference can reach the complement: the 3–7 % of its energy the fitted span misses, plus what the sample scaler moves there. A 45° in-span rotation is rejected by the complement in 0.35 (n = 200) and 0.32 (SEA-AD table) of replicates. At a weak stage signal the complement also contains whatever the fitted span missed of the program.
    • A span rejection means the difference has energy inside the fitted span. There delta, angle and shape say how it differs; the span part only says that it does. It is less powerful than the targeted statistics: magnitude 0.56 against delta's 0.74, a half-step bend 0.72 against shape's 1.00 (n = 200).
    • A non-rejection of complement or total is uninformative unless power has been shown at the design and for the kind of difference. On the SEA-AD blocks, a planted difference on 20 of 10,000 features at 2–3 within-stage SDs is rejected by no part above the null rate (0.02–0.05), as by angle. An energy test spreads over every coordinate, so a sparse difference does not register.
    • The in-span share (span_share) is descriptive. It must be read against the share a null analysis gives at the same rank and design, never against 0. Components beyond K − 1 follow the total covariance, where the noise's interaction energy also sits. At the plasmode anchor (rank 5) the null share is 0.24; at a two-component span at n = 200 it is 0.006.

    Why a ratio. RRPP permutes the reduced model's residuals, whose mean square is RSS/n, not RSS/(n − r). An energy statistic carries that shrinkage into its null, and summed over thousands of coordinates the null concentrates, so a few percent of scale becomes many null standard deviations. The pseudo-F computes its numerator and denominator from the same permuted residuals, so the scale cancels. At the plasmode anchor the uncorrected interaction SS rejects the null complement in 0.268 of replicates; the pseudo-F on the same draws rejects in 0.012.

    What it does not provide. No geometry: the split test says whether and where the groups differ, never how. It does not replace delta, angle or shape, and it does not find sparse differences: the per-feature interaction scan does. A group-aware space refitted per permutation is roadmap item 16's remaining follow-up (roadmap).

    Validation: split_validated (2026-11-05, report, 500 × 999 at the controls, 100 × 999 elsewhere, on the latent-space comparison's and the plasmode's own units):

    • Size holds on three simulated designs (the 18 control rates read 0.032–0.058, against a per-cell bound of 0.0695) and on the SEA-AD blocks at the case study's configuration (total 0.040, span 0.050, complement 0.012).
    • Out-of-span power. The complement rejects the 45° out-of-span rotation in 1.00 (n = 200) and 0.68 (SEA-AD table) of replicates, where angle reads 0.08 and 0.10.
    • Localization. As the rotation tilts out of the span, the span part falls (1.00 → 0.52) and the complement rises (0.35 → 1.00).
    • At SEA-AD's stage signal no part has power for any plant, and the complement holds size there (0.05–0.06), where concat's angle did not.

    Testing feature by feature: the interaction scan

    Every test above pools the group difference over many dimensions: delta, angle and shape over the fitted span, the split test's parts over their coordinates. A difference confined to a few features does not register in any of them. The SEA-AD plasmode's 20-feature plant was rejected at the null rate by all six. The per-feature interaction scan (motco.stats.interaction.interaction_feature_scan; motco analyze --interaction-scan) tests the group × stage interaction of each standardized feature on its own:

    • Per feature, the interaction F with the covariate-adjusted full and reduced trajectory models, the effect RRPP and the split test test.
    • Globally, the permutation p-value of the largest F over the features.
    • Feature by feature, a Westfall–Young step-down max-T adjusted p-value, which controls the familywise error rate over all scanned features. A hit is a feature with adjusted p ≤ 0.05.

    The draws are RRPP's Freedman–Lane draws at the analysis's seed, with its residual correction. The scan uses neither the latent space nor the bootstrap, so latent adjustment and attribution leave it unchanged.

    Reading a hit. A hit says that this feature's covariate-adjusted group difference changes across stages, with the familywise error rate over all scanned features controlled at 0.05. The per-stage differences in interaction_scan.csv (the second group minus the first, at each declared stage, in standardized units) show how. A hit does not say that the feature drives a latent trajectory difference, and correlated neighbours of a differing feature can be hits too.

    Reading a non-rejection. A scan without hits is uninformative unless power has been shown at the design and effect. At the plasmode's design (n = 80, six covariates, 10,000 features), a ramp to 2 within-stage SDs on 20 features is detected in 0.692 of replicates, and the median scan names one of the 20. A ramp to 3 SDs is detected in every replicate, and half the 20 are named.

    Why the maximum, not the sum. The split test's complement sums interaction energy over thousands of coordinates, and the noise in that sum grows with their number, so a few features' energy is lost. A maximum pays only for the search: its threshold grows like the logarithm of the feature count. The scan therefore targets differences on few features, and the complement targets differences spread over many. The simulation shows the division (report, §6). The scan is weak where a difference spreads thinly: magnitude 0.09 against delta's 0.74, and a weak bend on the SEA-AD table 0.20 against shape's 0.70. With 498 features the search is cheap, so the scan also matches or beats the complement on a 45° out-of-span rotation (1.00 / 0.96 against 1.00 / 0.68). The complement still wins at half that rotation on the SEA-AD table (0.31 against 0.16).

    The scan, attribution and the split test make different claims.

    • Attribution ranks features by their contribution to a latent difference that was already detected, and makes no error-rate claim.
    • The scan tests each feature's interaction directly, under familywise control, without a latent space. On the plasmode its 20 largest F hold 0.473 / 0.887 of the plant at 2 / 3 SDs, against attribution's 0.227 / 0.432. The plasmode's attribution verdict (attribution_leg_failed) is unchanged: the scan is a new test with its own predeclared rule, not a rescue of attribution's.
    • The split test says whether the groups differ by stage inside or outside the fitted span; the scan says on which features.

    What it does not provide. No geometry: the scan names features whose stage pattern differs between the groups, never how the trajectories differ. It is not a driver analysis. Its F is the plain per-feature F, not a variance-moderated one. Its familywise threshold is common to all features, and on the SEA-AD export one block's null is heavier: ATAC gives 0.618 of the null maxima with half the features, so the threshold leans on ATAC. The by-block scan (below) gives each block its own share of the error.

    Validation: scan_validated (2026-11-09, report, on the split-test validation's own units, which it reproduced exactly):

    • Size holds on three simulated designs (the six control rates read 0.048–0.058, against a per-cell bound of 0.0695) and on the SEA-AD blocks at the case study's configuration (0.042).
    • Familywise control. Under the plasmode's partial null, a feature outside the planted set is a hit in 0.042 of replicates at 2 and at 3 SDs.
    • Sparse gain. At 3 SDs the scan rejects in 1.000 of replicates, against 0.016–0.048 for delta, angle, shape and the three split parts on the same units. At 3 SDs, 0.995 of its hits are planted features.

    Scanning by block

    One maximum over every feature lets the block that most often gives the largest null F set every block's bar. A block with many features, or with heavier-tailed noise, takes most of the familywise error, and a small block's features face a threshold set by the large block's search. The by-block scan (motco analyze --interaction-scan-by-block; API) runs the same step-down within each declared block, on the same draws, and tests each of the K blocks at 0.05/K.

    Reading a by-block hit. The claim is the joint scan's: this feature's covariate-adjusted group difference changes across stages, with the familywise error over all scanned features controlled at 0.05. What changes is who carries the error. Each block gets 0.05/K whatever its size or noise, so each block has about the same chance of a false hit.

    When to use it. When the blocks differ in size, or their noise is known to differ. With equal blocks it mostly moves detection from the heavier-tailed block to the other at the same total. Only K = 2 has been measured; with many small blocks each gets only 0.05/K.

    Validation: by_block_validated (2026-11-10, report, on the interaction-scan validation's units, whose joint scan it reproduced exactly, plus an unequal export with RNA cut to 500 of its 5,000 features):

    • Size and familywise control hold. The six simulation controls read 0.046–0.058. On real noise the global rate reads 0.040 (SEA-AD export) and 0.044 (unequal export), and a feature outside the planted set is a hit in 0.040 and 0.044 of units.
    • The error is shared. At the unequal export's anchor the joint scan puts its false hits almost all in ATAC (0.052 of units against RNA's 0.004); by block they read 0.030 and 0.016.
    • No global power is lost, and the small block gains. On the unequal export the global rate at 2 SDs rises from 0.760 to 0.892. RNA recall rises from 0.069 to 0.158 (2 SDs) and from 0.570 to 0.755 (3 SDs), while ATAC's falls from 0.076 to 0.058 and from 0.575 to 0.500. On the SEA-AD export the two blocks' mean recall is unchanged (0.059 at 2 SDs).
    • Bonferroni costs little at K = 2. The block maxima are weakly correlated (median Spearman 0.18–0.20 on the exports), and a minP combination would have rejected at most 2 more units of 500.

    The joint scan stays the default; the by-block combination is opt-in.
